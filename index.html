NJÖRD & Not clean enough:
artefacts for a collective discussion.

NJÖRD interrogates a practice made possible by technology: bending living matter to an aesthetic whim. Not Clean Enough interrogates an aesthetic presented as natural and as the pinnacle of an ideal lifestyle, yet unattainable. On the level of content, the projects share nothing in common. In both artifacts, however, the same mechanism is at work. The public is not offered an argument to read, but a task to carry out.

Configuring a colour, classifying a comment: it is in this shift, from reading to gesture, that the content becomes open to discussion. The visitor’s gesture does not end with the act itself. It generates a trace, and this trace becomes the material on which collective discussion is built. My work has consisted in defining how that trace is generated, collected, made visible, and returned to those who produced it.

NJÖRD

Shape your Desire.

( Scope )Speculative Design, Brand Design, UX & UI Design.

( Context )Final Synthesis Studio C1, Politecnico di Milano.

( Exhibition )Raw Scenarios: reflections on human overconsumption.

( Traces )286 salmon configured, recorded in the server log.

NJÖRD is a speculative design project that stems from real-world practice. The flesh of farmed salmon is white: its orange color comes from crustaceans that the salmon cannot eat. Producers color the flesh using dyes added to the feed, selected according to DSM-Firmenich’s SalmoFan, the industry’s color standard.

SalmoFan — DSM-Firmenich colour standard, shades 20 to 34.

The project envisions a physical console that extends this choice to consumers, offering a variety of colors and textures to customize the flesh of their own farmed salmon. The device thus challenges the practice of reshaping living organisms to satisfy an aesthetic whim, exposing a choice that remains the sole domain of producers.

Screen — the salmon configured by the visitors.

Visual identity wasn’t decoration—it’s what made it work. Had NJÖRD read as protest, no one would have believed the speculation. So I decided to set up a fake fish business: industry vocabulary, certification-grade cleanliness, a chain that never explains itself. The hard part was subtraction: any irony broke the spell.

Identity — logotype, typographic system and animated marks.

The applications that carry the identity are the ones the service actually needs: the pack the fillet is sealed in, the cooler bag it travels in, the ice packs that keep that bag cold. Nothing here is a showcase piece. It is the delivery chain doing its job, and it is what makes the service—and with it the identity—credible.

Packaging — vacuum pack for the fillet.

Box — front claim and product label.

Ice pack — moulded shell, front and back.

Shipping box — pull strip along the front edge.

I handled the machine’s software, working with the hardware team. The interface had
to make configuring a living being feel like picking a paint sample. Every configuration was logged—not for research, but because the service had to process orders. That log became the day’s only record.

Console — the screen above the ordering unit.

Console — details on moving the knob.

Interface — four screens of the ordering flow.

When the interaction with the machine ends, the service moves onto the customer’s own device. A QR code leads straight to it: the configuration just made can be consulted there, colour and texture alongside the data of that specific salmon. It opens on a phone as it does on a computer, and the choice follows the customer home.

App — the salmon as the customer receives it.

App — the salmon and its data sheet.

Website — the home page of the business.

Raw Scenarios ran on 22 June 2024 at DotDotDot, a Milan-based studio. Eleven groups, one day, a student-organized exhibition with a mostly academic audience. The high-tech fish market environment we designed around the console never made it in—resources, time, space. The identity did the job the space would have.

Fish shop — render of the interior, console at the centre.

Console — the two disks that set colour and texture.

Visitors — a salmon configured at Raw Scenarios.

Console — in use at Raw Scenarios, 22 June 2024.

Speculative design offers no model for what makes a project of this kind effective, so
I built four frameworks, one per dimension. They worked as instruments and failed as a reading: the judgements of 286 visitors became scores, and the scores an average, which removes divergence instead of recording it. The next evaluation area is built against this.

The full material is in the Bachelor’s thesis, written in Italian.

Not clean enough

( Scope )Data Design, Brand Design, UX & UI Design.

( Context )Final Synthesis Studio C3, Politecnico di Milano.

( Exhibition )Stream, Beef, Vibes, Milan, February 2026

( Traces )104 classifications collected at the evaluation area

Not Clean Enough is an inquiry into the comments under the TikTok videos of clean girl influencers, specifically into those stating the impossibility of achieving the aesthetic promoted. The clean aesthetic is a minimal trend born in 2022 and spread rapidly across the U.S.A. and the U.K. It promotes an essential and apparently natural lifestyle.

Mapping wall — the comments excluded from the clean girl aesthetic.

The project began within a group of four people and started from three inquiries carried out in parallel on the same phenomenon. The first identifies which actions and which objects make up the aesthetic of the trend. The second estimates the real cost of what the videos show. The third examines how the public perceives that ideal.

Dataset — the actions shown in the videos, timed. Link to the dataset ↗

Dataset — the products shown in the videos and their cost. Link to the dataset ↗

Dataset — the comments, classified by category. Link to the dataset ↗

The third inquiry produced the most solid outcome. First two describe the phenomenon; in the comments, instead, those who observe it speak for themselves. The work therefore concentrates on a single question: what do those who would like to follow the trend, cannot, declare they lack? Four categories: appearance, status, motivation, provenance.

Dataset — the comments and the insecurity each one names. Link to the dataset ↗

The organisation of the comments is followed by a manual classification, carried out on the basis of keywords. The visualisation consists of seventeen vertical columns printed on banners: the length of each corresponds to the number of people who declared that same lack, and makes all the categories immediately comparable with one another.

Installation — the mapping wall and the project posters.

Banners — each comment with its expression underlined.

The exhibition path opens with a video in four parts, one for each of the categories identified. Each section is introduced by the avatar of a real clean girl influencer, generated with AI. The avatar anticipates a collection of videos that visually show the lack to which that category corresponds. It is the first element that the public encounters.

Each project in the exhibition had its evaluation area. The one for Not Clean Enough consists of a table with markers, stickers and cards, with the instructions printed directly on its surface. Facing the table stands a board divided into columns, one for each of the four categories plus an Unsure column, where each card is then posted.

Evaluation area — the table and the board of columns.

Four steps are printed on the table, in the order they are to be followed.

One. Take a single card.
Two. Read the underlined expressions.
Three. Highlight the one that names the lack.
Four. Post the card under the category matching the colour you used.

Table — the instructions printed on the surface.

Card — the question printed on every card.

Not Clean Enough was shown at Stream, Beef, Vibes at Stecca 3.0, an exhibition space in Milan, where 104 participants took part in the evaluation. The evaluation area itself collected no judgements: it handed each visitor a set of uncategorised comments and asked them to apply to those the same constructive logic as the visualisation itself.

Opening — the evaluation area in use.

Cards — the expressions highlighted by the visitors.

Appearance — the cards posted under that category.

The cards filled in at the evaluation area were digitised at a later stage, in the context
of an internship at the DensityDesign Lab. The analysis of the results shows that some categories are more ambiguous than others: provenance in particular, often attributed
to comments that do not belong to it. Of the 104 cards, 93 are correct and 11 incorrect.

Dataset — the 104 cards, digitised and checked.

The mechanism is visible in a single case. On each card some expressions are underlined,
and those are the ones to be assessed. In the comment “The products cost 5000 Mexican pesos. We are poor” both pointed to Status, but the card ended up under Provenance: the reading had been shifted by the word Mexican, printed there but not underlined.

Error map — the incorrect cards, by type of misreading.
